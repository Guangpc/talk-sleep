# iOS Bedtime Voice Companion: Primary-Source Constraints

> **Research note — 2026-09-03.** This is a source-backed constraints inventory for a commercial iOS MVP. It does not choose a product design, make a legal conclusion, or determine whether a particular implementation will pass App Review. Apple’s live [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) and platform documentation control at submission time; the cited GDPR and California sources apply only when their jurisdictional scope and facts are met.

## Scope and reading guide

The proposed capability set considered here is: user-provided chat screenshots and audio; real-time microphone input; speech recognition; text-to-speech or voice-style simulation; audio while the app is backgrounded or the device is locked; transcripts; sleep-time inference; and minimizing cloud storage. The findings below separate **requirements stated by a source** from **implementation questions that follow from applying the source to those capabilities**. The latter are not product decisions.

## 1. App Store Review Guidelines

### 1.1 Privacy policy, permission, minimization, and deletion

Apple requires every app to link a privacy policy in App Store Connect metadata and make it easily accessible inside the app. The policy must clearly identify what data the app or service collects, how it collects it, all uses, third parties that receive it, retention/deletion policies, and how a user can revoke consent or request deletion. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

**Implications to resolve for this MVP (without prescribing the resolution):**

- The policy and in-app disclosures need to distinguish at least microphone recordings, speech-recognition input, generated or saved audio, screenshots/photos, extracted text, transcripts, account identifiers, diagnostics, and any sleep-related estimate or other inference if those data are collected or retained. Apple’s App Privacy documentation says developers must describe collected data, whether it is linked to the user/device, and whether it is used for tracking. ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))
- Any cloud model, speech service, storage provider, analytics SDK, moderation vendor, or voice provider receiving user data needs to be named or described consistently with the policy and App Privacy answers; Apple expressly includes third-party SDKs and related entities in the disclosure obligation. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))
- “Cloud minimized” needs a data-flow statement: what leaves the device, for which operation, whether the provider retains or trains on it, how long it remains available, and how deletion propagates. Apple’s requirement is to describe actual collection, use, sharing, retention, and deletion, not an architectural aspiration. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- If the app creates accounts, Apple requires an in-app account-deletion option subject to the exceptions and process described in §5.1.1(v); account deletion must address associated user content and backend records, not just sign the user out. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(v)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Apple account-deletion announcement](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=12m75xbj))

Apple’s permission rule requires an app to obtain consent before collecting user or usage data, and its data-minimization rule limits collection to data directly relevant to the app’s core functionality. The permission request must explain what the app will do with the data; changing the purpose or sharing it in a way not covered by the original permission requires further consent under the Guidelines. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(ii)–(iii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

For this capability set, permission boundaries should be reviewed separately for importing a screenshot, selecting an audio file, recording live microphone input, speech recognition, sending content to a third-party AI, saving a transcript, and deriving or retaining sleep-related inferences. This is a review question derived from Apple’s purpose/permission requirements, not a conclusion that one combined prompt is or is not sufficient. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1(ii)–(iii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 1.2 Third-party AI and sharing

The current Guidelines require clear disclosure of where personal data will be shared with third parties, including third-party AI, and explicit permission before that sharing. This is directly relevant if screenshots, recordings, transcripts, prompts, or inferences are sent to an external model or voice service. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.2(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

Apple also requires the app’s use of data to be consistent with the permission and privacy policy, and restricts use of collected data for purposes unrelated to the app’s core functionality unless the user consents. A model vendor’s secondary uses therefore need to be included in vendor due diligence and user-facing disclosures. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 1.3 User-generated, generated, and objectionable content

If the product exposes content to other users or otherwise operates as a user-generated-content or social service, Apple requires objectionable-content prevention, a reporting mechanism, the ability to block abusive users, and published contact information; services used primarily for pornography, threats, bullying, or certain random/anonymous-chat experiences do not belong on the App Store. ([App Review Guidelines §1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

Even where content is private, Apple’s safety rules prohibit objectionable content and review can consider the content the app displays or generates. For a bedtime companion, the review surface includes imported screenshot/audio content, model outputs, voice outputs, crisis or self-harm conversations, sexual content, and content that could encourage physical harm. The Guidelines’ safety and medical/health sections should be assessed if the app makes sleep, mental-health, or medical claims. ([App Review Guidelines §§1.1, 1.4, 5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

If the app allows users to publish or share generated voice or transcripts, moderation and complaint handling should cover both uploaded material and model-generated material. That is a conditional application of §1.2, not a determination that a private assistant is a UGC service. ([App Review Guidelines §1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 1.4 Rights in screenshots, recordings, and voices

Apple’s intellectual-property rule says an app must contain only content that the developer created or is licensed to use, and apps that use or display third-party material must have permission or another applicable right. A screenshot may contain another person’s messages, name, likeness, copyrighted images, or confidential information; a recording may contain another person’s voice. The app’s terms and review notes should not assume that the uploader has authority to grant all required rights. ([App Review Guidelines §5.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

Apple’s Guidelines do not define a general “voice-style simulation” safe harbor. Accordingly, whether a user supplies a reference recording, selects a recognizable person’s style, or uses a synthetic voice remains a rights, consent, misrepresentation, and safety question that must be assessed for the relevant jurisdictions and provider terms; the Apple-facing constraint is to avoid unauthorized third-party material and misleading or harmful content. ([App Review Guidelines §§1.1, 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 1.5 Background audio and completeness

Apple’s performance rules require a submission to be a final, functional build, tested on-device, with a live backend when required. If login is required, review must receive demo-account information or an approved demo mode; review notes should explain non-obvious AI, audio, or permission flows. ([App Review Guidelines §2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

The Guidelines require apps to use public APIs and only use background modes for their intended purposes. The audio background mode therefore needs to correspond to actual user-facing media playback or recording behavior; it should not be used as a generic keep-alive for inference, uploads, analytics, or arbitrary server work. ([App Review Guidelines §2.5.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes))

### 1.6 Payment and subscription review

If the app unlocks digital functionality, AI usage, premium voices, saved transcripts, or other digital content in the iOS app, Apple’s default rule is to use In-App Purchase rather than a proprietary unlock mechanism. Auto-renewable subscriptions must follow the App Store subscription rules and provide the required product information, purchase/restore/manage paths, and functional review access. ([App Review Guidelines §§3.1.1–3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 1.7 Health and medical positioning

Apple’s health rules prohibit using health or fitness data for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining outside the allowed purposes, and the Guidelines impose additional expectations on apps that provide medical information or health-related functionality. A “sleep-time inference” is therefore a claim/data-classification question: its wording, inputs, outputs, storage, and downstream use should be assessed before selecting App Store metadata or a Health category. ([App Review Guidelines §§1.4, 5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

## 2. Native iOS API and execution constraints

### 2.1 Microphone permission and recording

Apple’s audio-session guidance states that an app must ask for and receive user permission before recording audio; without permission, recording produces silence. The app can request permission explicitly with `requestRecordPermission`, and the user can change the decision in Settings. ([Audio Session Programming Guide — Requesting Permission](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/RequestingPermission/RequestingPermission.html), [`requestRecordPermission`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/requestrecordpermission(_:)))

The app must include a clear `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` value in its `Info.plist`; Apple’s privacy-sensitive-data rejection guidance lists the microphone usage-description key and the permission API as the relevant resolution. The copy should describe the actual live-recording/transcription/audio purpose, not a generic “for functionality” statement. ([Apple QA1937](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1937/_index.html), [`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsmicrophoneusagedescription))

A design that records while the display is locked or the app is backgrounded must separately test the audio-session category, background mode, route, interruption behavior, user indicators, and stop conditions. The cited Apple material establishes the permission and audio-session requirements; it does not grant permission to record another person or to record indefinitely without user awareness. ([Audio Session Programming Guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/), [App Review Guidelines §§2.5.1, 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 2.2 Speech recognition and on-device processing

The Speech framework exposes an authorization request and status for speech recognition. The app should request this before starting recognition and handle denied, restricted, unavailable, and locale/model failure states. ([Asking Permission to Use Speech Recognition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/asking-permission-to-use-speech-recognition), [`SFSpeechRecognizer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognizer))

`SFSpeechRecognitionRequest.requiresOnDeviceRecognition` requests that recognition occur on the device; Apple documents that this is only possible when on-device recognition is supported, which can be checked with `supportsOnDeviceRecognition`. Requiring on-device recognition can fail on unsupported configurations, so a cloud-minimized claim needs a tested fallback policy and an accurate disclosure if audio can otherwise leave the device. ([`requiresOnDeviceRecognition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognitionrequest/requiresondevicerecognition), [`supportsOnDeviceRecognition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognizer/supportsondevicerecognition))

Speech recognition is not the same as transcript retention. Apple’s API choice controls where recognition runs; the product/backend still determines whether raw audio, partial results, final transcripts, prompts, or derived inferences are saved or sent to other services. The privacy policy and App Privacy answers must describe those actual flows. ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 2.3 Playback, synthesis, and audio sessions

`AVAudioSession` represents the app’s audio behavior and provides categories including recording, playback, and `playAndRecord`; the latter supports simultaneous input and output. The audio session must be configured and activated in accordance with the chosen behavior, and the app must handle interruptions and route changes. ([`AVAudioSession`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession), [`playAndRecord`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playandrecord), [Audio Session Programming Guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/))

Apple’s `AVSpeechSynthesizer` synthesizes speech from text using system voices. If the MVP instead calls a cloud TTS or voice-conversion provider, that provider is a third party receiving the relevant text/audio or voice reference and must be reflected in sharing, consent, retention, security, and App Privacy analysis. ([`AVSpeechSynthesizer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechsynthesizer), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 2.4 Background and locked-screen audio

Apple’s media-playback guidance uses an audio session configured for playback and the `audio` value in `UIBackgroundModes` to allow audio to continue when the app is not foregrounded. Apple’s technical Q&A separately documents the configuration needed for playback when the screen is locked. ([Configuring your app for media playback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/configuring-your-app-for-media-playback), [QA1668](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1668/), [QA1626](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1626/), [`UIBackgroundModes`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/uibackgroundmodes))

Playback and live microphone capture are different cases: a playback session does not authorize input, and a `playAndRecord` or recording configuration does not by itself guarantee that a locked-screen conversation will continue. The implementation should test each state—foreground playback, background playback, locked playback, foreground capture, and any background capture—against the actual audio session and background mode. ([Audio Session Programming Guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/), [App Review Guidelines §2.5.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

Apple’s background-execution documentation describes permitted background modes and warns that each mode is intended for a specific use. A continuously active microphone, continuous speech recognition, network inference, or upload loop should not be represented to App Review as “background audio” unless the user-facing behavior genuinely fits that mode. ([Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes), [`UIBackgroundModes`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/uibackgroundmodes))

## 3. Apple privacy disclosures and supply-chain requirements

### 3.1 App Privacy details (Nutrition Labels)

Apple requires developers to provide App Privacy details in App Store Connect for new apps and updates. The product page communicates data categories collected, whether data is linked to the user or device, and whether it is used for tracking; third-party code’s collection and use must also be included. ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))

For this feature set, the inventory exercise should explicitly consider: photos or screenshots; audio recordings and voice references; free-form text, prompts, and transcripts; account/contact identifiers; diagnostics; and sleep-related health data or inferences. The exact App Store category depends on what is collected, whether it is linked, the purpose, and whether the app retains or shares it; the label should not be inferred from the UI name alone. ([Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/), [Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/))

Apple distinguishes tracking from ordinary first-party processing and requires user permission through App Tracking Transparency when the app tracks the user across apps or websites owned by other companies. An AI companion that does not track may still need App Privacy disclosures, but ATT status cannot be decided without auditing analytics, advertising, attribution, device identifiers, and SDK behavior. ([App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))

### 3.2 Privacy manifests and required-reason API declarations

Apple’s privacy-manifest mechanism lets an app and its third-party SDKs describe privacy practices, including collected-data types and tracking, and helps identify an SDK’s use of required-reason APIs. The app team should inspect every SDK used for networking, analytics, speech, TTS, image processing, storage, and attribution, then reconcile manifests with the App Privacy answers and privacy policy. ([Privacy manifest files](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files), [Adding a privacy manifest to your app or third-party SDK](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/adding-a-privacy-manifest-to-your-app-or-third-party-sdk), [WWDC23: Get started with privacy manifests](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10060/))

A privacy manifest does not replace runtime permission prompts, user-facing notices, App Store Privacy details, contracts with service providers, or deletion controls. It is one declaration layer in Apple’s privacy-supply-chain process; the implementation must keep all layers consistent. ([Privacy manifest files](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/), [App Review Guidelines §5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

## 4. General regulatory primary sources

This section is intentionally limited to high-level, source-text principles. It is not a jurisdictional applicability analysis. Counsel should determine scope, roles, lawful bases, notices, international transfers, children’s obligations, biometric/health classifications, and sector-specific rules for the launch markets.

### 4.1 GDPR (conditional EU/EEA applicability)

The GDPR applies according to its territorial-scope rules, including offering services to people in the Union and, in certain circumstances, monitoring their behavior. ([GDPR Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The core processing principles include lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimization; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; and accountability. A bedtime companion should therefore be able to state the purpose for each input and output, collect only what that purpose needs, set retention periods, secure the data, and demonstrate compliance. ([GDPR Art. 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

Processing needs a lawful basis such as consent, contract necessity, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests, as applicable to the facts. Consent must be demonstrable, distinguishable from other matters, intelligible and accessible, and as easy to withdraw as to give; where consent is relied on for optional recording, third-party AI sharing, or voice-reference processing, those operations should not be hidden in unrelated acceptance. ([GDPR Art. 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [GDPR Art. 7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

Health data is a special category, and biometric data is special-category data when it results from specific technical processing relating to physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics that allows or confirms unique identification. A sleep-related inference may be health data depending on its content and context; a voice recording is not automatically biometric data merely because it is a voice, but voice processing for unique identification can meet the biometric definition. Special-category processing generally needs both an Article 6 basis and an Article 9 condition, such as explicit consent where applicable. ([GDPR Art. 4(4), 4(14), 4(15)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [GDPR Art. 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The controller must provide information about purposes, legal bases, recipients, transfers, retention, rights, and automated decision-making as required by the notice provisions. The app should map those disclosures to screenshots/audio, recognition, transcript generation, TTS/voice conversion, storage, deletion, and sleep inference rather than using a generic “AI improves your experience” description. ([GDPR Arts. 13–14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

Data subjects have rights that can include access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, and portability, subject to the Regulation’s conditions and exceptions. A deletion workflow should cover source files, cached uploads, transcripts, embeddings or derived features, generated audio, model-vendor copies, backups, and account records to the extent the controller is responsible for them. ([GDPR Arts. 15–20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The GDPR requires data protection by design and by default, processor contracts with required terms, and appropriate technical and organisational security measures. For a cloud-minimized MVP, these principles make local processing, short-lived transport, encryption, access controls, vendor restrictions, deletion propagation, and auditable retention choices relevant engineering questions; they do not prescribe one architecture. ([GDPR Arts. 25, 28, 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

A data-protection impact assessment is required before processing likely to result in a high risk to individuals, including systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing or large-scale special-category processing. Whether sleep-time inference, voice analysis, or transcript analysis reaches that threshold depends on scale, technology, purpose, and effects, so the threshold should be documented before launch. ([GDPR Art. 35](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

If an automated result produces legal effects or similarly significant effects, the GDPR’s automated-decision provisions may apply; ordinary personalization or a bedtime suggestion is not automatically such a decision. The product team should assess the actual consequence and human-review path rather than labeling every AI output a legally significant automated decision. ([GDPR Art. 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The GDPR defines personal data broadly as information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person and defines processing to include operations such as collection, recording, storage, alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure/transmission, restriction, erasure, or destruction. A voice, transcript, account-linked screenshot, or sleep result can therefore be personal data when it relates to an identifiable person, and sending ephemeral audio to a vendor is still processing even if it is later deleted. ([GDPR Art. 4(1)–(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The GDPR’s definition of profiling includes automated processing used to evaluate or predict personal aspects such as health, preferences, interests, reliability, behavior, location, or movements. A sleep-quality or sleep-disorder prediction may be profiling and may also be health data depending on its content and context; a generic bedtime timestamp is not automatically health data. ([GDPR Art. 4(4), 4(15)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

For children, the GDPR’s information-society-service consent rule defaults to age 16 and permits Member States to lower that age to no less than 13. A child-directed or knowingly child-facing bedtime service therefore needs a jurisdiction-specific age/consent analysis. ([GDPR Art. 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

The GDPR also requires notification of a personal-data breach to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours unless an exception applies; when a breach is likely to result in a high risk to people, affected data subjects must also be informed without undue delay subject to the Article 34 exceptions. Incident response should include cloud vendors and derived data, not only the app database. ([GDPR Arts. 33–34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

Transfers of personal data outside the European Economic Area must satisfy the GDPR’s Chapter V conditions, such as an adequacy decision or an appropriate safeguard, subject to the Regulation’s rules and exceptions. A “cloud minimized” design still needs a transfer analysis whenever audio, screenshots, transcripts, or inferences cross a relevant border. ([GDPR Arts. 44–49](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))

### 4.2 California CCPA/CPRA (conditional California applicability)

California’s statutory definition of personal information expressly reaches, subject to its qualifiers, audio/electronic/visual information, biometric information, and inferences used to create a profile; its biometric definition includes voice recordings from which a voiceprint can be extracted, and sensitive personal information includes health information and biometric information processed to uniquely identify a person. The classification depends on the statutory definitions and facts, not merely on calling a data item “voice” or “wellness.” ([California Civil Code §1798.140](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=1.81.5.&part=4.))

California’s Civil Code requires covered businesses to provide notice at collection and disclose the categories of personal information collected, purposes, and other information specified by statute; it also provides rights including access, deletion, and correction, subject to statutory exceptions. The statute’s applicability, thresholds, definitions, response deadlines, and exemptions must be checked against the business and user facts. ([California Civil Code §§1798.100, 1798.105, 1798.106](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5), [California Attorney General — CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa))

The CCPA/CPRA includes rules concerning sale or sharing of personal information, opt-out rights, and limiting certain uses of sensitive personal information. If an SDK, ad-tech partner, model provider, or data broker receives data in a way that meets those statutory definitions, the corresponding notice, opt-out, contract, and signal obligations need a California-specific analysis. ([California Civil Code §§1798.120–1798.121](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5), [California Attorney General — CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa))

For this MVP, California review should inventory screenshots, audio, voice references, transcripts, account identifiers, and inferences as potentially personal information where they meet the statutory definitions, and should document recipients and retention. The exact classification and whether a transfer is a sale or sharing are fact-dependent under the statute; do not infer them from “no ads” or “cloud minimized” alone. ([California Civil Code §§1798.140, 1798.100](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5))

California’s notice-at-collection rule covers categories, purposes, sale/share status, and separate sensitive-personal-information categories and purposes; the statute also requires collection, use, retention, and sharing to be reasonably necessary and proportionate to the disclosed purposes. The access request rules cover categories, sources, purposes, recipients, and specific pieces; the deletion right generally requires notifying service providers, contractors, and third parties to delete where the statute requires it. ([California Civil Code §§1798.100, 1798.105, 1798.110, 1798.115](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=1.81.5.&part=4.))

The CCPA/CPRA is not a universal consent-before-collection statute. Its controls include notice, access, deletion, correction, opt-out of sale or sharing, limits on certain sensitive-personal-information uses, and non-discrimination, with request methods and generally applicable response periods in §1798.130. Whether a constrained AI service provider’s receipt is a sale or sharing depends on the contract and actual independent retention, training, or use; the business thresholds and operative version must be checked for the launch date. ([California Civil Code §§1798.106, 1798.120–1798.135](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=1.81.5.&part=4.), [California Attorney General — CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa))

## 5. Evidence and launch-review checklist

The following are verification questions, not product decisions:

1. **Data map:** For each screenshot, audio item, microphone frame, recognition result, transcript, generated audio file, voice reference, inference, identifier, log, cache, backup, and vendor request, can the team state collection point, purpose, recipient, retention, deletion behavior, linkage, and tracking status? ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [GDPR Arts. 5, 13–14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj))
2. **Permission UX:** Are microphone and speech-recognition permissions requested at the point of need with accurate purpose strings, and does every optional third-party AI/voice transfer have a clear disclosure and consent path where required? ([Apple QA1937](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1937/_index.html), [Apple speech permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/asking-permission-to-use-speech-recognition), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
3. **On-device claim:** Does the app verify `supportsOnDeviceRecognition`, set `requiresOnDeviceRecognition` when that is the intended guarantee, and accurately describe any fallback that sends audio/text to a service? ([`requiresOnDeviceRecognition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognitionrequest/requiresondevicerecognition), [`supportsOnDeviceRecognition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognizer/supportsondevicerecognition), [App Review Guidelines §5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
4. **Background behavior:** On real devices, does playback continue correctly after backgrounding and locking, while capture and recognition stop or continue only in an intentionally supported, disclosed, and reviewable state? ([QA1668](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1668/), [QA1626](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1626/), [App Review Guidelines §§2.5.1, 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
5. **Content rights and safety:** Can the uploader represent that imported screenshots/audio and any voice reference may be processed, and are model outputs screened and handled consistently with the safety, UGC, and rights rules? ([App Review Guidelines §§1.1–1.2, 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
6. **Deletion:** Does account/data deletion remove or expire originals, transcripts, generated audio, derived features, vendor copies, and backups to the extent promised, and can the app explain exceptions? ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [GDPR Arts. 5, 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [California Civil Code §1798.105](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5))
7. **SDK/build evidence:** Have the team audited privacy manifests, required-reason APIs, SDK data practices, ATS/ATT behavior, App Privacy answers, and privacy-policy wording as one consistent set? ([Privacy manifests](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files), [Apple App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))
8. **Review package:** Is the final build stable, backend-enabled, and accompanied by reviewer credentials, test content, and notes that explain permission, audio, locked-screen, AI, subscription, and safety flows? ([App Review Guidelines §§2.1, 3.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

## 5. Current Apple clarifications to verify before implementation

### 5.1 Recording indication is separate from authorization

Apple’s current §2.5.14 requires explicit user consent and a clear visual and/or audible indication when an app records, logs, or otherwise makes a record of user activity, including microphone, camera, screen recordings, and other inputs. For any live-listening or recording turn, treat the in-app listening/recording state and a user-visible stop control as separate review requirements; do not assume that the system microphone indicator alone satisfies the app-level indication requirement. ([App Review Guidelines §2.5.14](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.2 Background services are purpose-limited

The current Guidelines’ multitasking rule is §2.5.4: background services may be used only for their intended purposes, such as audio playback or VoIP. The audio background mode is therefore not a general keep-alive for always-on microphone listening, arbitrary background computation, or an indefinitely live cloud-AI/network pipeline. ([App Review Guidelines §2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes))

The public-API rule is separately stated in §2.5.1. Where this report previously described both points together, read them as two checks: use public APIs under §2.5.1, and justify the selected background service under §2.5.4. ([App Review Guidelines §§2.5.1, 2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.3 Audio API details and current documentation

For an audio-session recorder, Apple’s direct permission API is `AVAudioSession.requestRecordPermission(_:)`; an `AVCaptureDevice` authorization API is relevant only when the implementation uses an AVCaptureDevice capture pipeline. Both require the appropriate usage description, and the exact pipeline should determine which source and key are used. ([`requestRecordPermission`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/requestrecordpermission(_:)), [`AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/requestaccess(for:completionhandler:)), [requesting authorization to capture and save media](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/requesting-authorization-to-capture-and-save-media), [`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsmicrophoneusagedescription))

Apple documents `playback` as the audio-session category for media playback, including playback while the Ring/Silent switch is set to silent or the screen is locked; continuing when the app transitions to the background additionally requires the `audio` background mode. `playAndRecord` is the category for simultaneous input and output, while `record` is input-only. ([`playback`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playback), [`playAndRecord`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playandrecord), [`record`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/record), [`UIBackgroundModes`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/uibackgroundmodes))

The audio-session implementation should configure the category, mode, and options for the actual interaction, activate the session only when recording or playback is about to occur, and handle interruptions and route changes. Background configuration does not promise immunity from calls, alarms, route changes, suspension, termination, or network failure. ([`AVAudioSession`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession), [Handling audio interruptions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/handling-audio-interruptions), [Responding to audio route changes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/responding-to-audio-route-changes))

Apple’s Speech sources distinguish live microphone-buffer recognition from file recognition. Speech authorization is separate from microphone authorization, and the speech usage-description key is `NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription`; Apple’s permission and live-audio documentation should be followed for the selected request type. ([`SFSpeechAudioBufferRecognitionRequest`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechaudiobufferrecognitionrequest), [`SFSpeechURLRecognitionRequest`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechurlrecognitionrequest), [Recognizing speech in live audio](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/recognizing-speech-in-live-audio), [Asking permission to use speech recognition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/asking-permission-to-use-speech-recognition), [`NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsspeechrecognitionusagedescription))

Apple notes in its Speech authorization guidance that speech recognition may capture the user’s voice and send it to Apple’s servers for processing. That Apple-controlled path is distinct from any separately selected remote STT, model, or TTS vendor and should be represented accurately in the app’s disclosures. ([Asking permission to use speech recognition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/asking-permission-to-use-speech-recognition), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

`AVSpeechSynthesizer` speaks `AVSpeechUtterance` text and provides speech-control/delegate APIs; it does not itself grant background execution or a microphone permission. File-based recording and playback use separate AVFAudio primitives. ([`AVSpeechSynthesizer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechsynthesizer), [`AVSpeechUtterance`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechutterance), [`AVAudioRecorder`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiorecorder), [`AVAudioPlayer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayer))

### 5.4 Importing screenshots with the least library access

For a screenshot-import flow, Apple documents an out-of-process photo picker that can give the user a privacy-preserving selection experience without requiring broad photo-library access. If the app directly accesses the photo library instead, it needs the appropriate `NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription` purpose string. Selecting a screenshot through a picker does not by itself authorize uploading that screenshot to a third-party AI service; that transfer remains subject to the separate disclosure and permission analysis under §5.1.2. ([Selecting photos and videos in iOS](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/photokit/selecting-photos-and-videos-in-ios), [Delivering an enhanced privacy experience in your Photos app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/photokit/delivering-an-enhanced-privacy-experience-in-your-photos-app), [`NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsphotolibraryusagedescription), [App Review Guidelines §5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.5 Apple’s precise App Privacy taxonomy

Apple’s App Privacy Details page identifies “Photos or Videos” as users’ photos or videos, “Audio Data” as users’ voice or sound recordings, and “Other User Content” as other user-generated content; Apple specifically gives free-form text and voice recordings as examples that may be saved through user-facing fields. The team should classify screenshots, recordings, extracted text, transcripts, prompts, and generated/voice-reference material according to the actual data collected, link status, purpose, and third-party practices. ([App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/))

Apple defines collection for App Privacy Details as transmitting data off-device and allowing the app or its partner to access it for longer than needed to service the request; on-device-only processing is not collection for this label, and an off-device derived result is considered separately. An off-device request that is immediately discarded may fall within an App Privacy disclosure exception, but a vendor’s retention of recordings, transcripts, screenshots, embeddings, logs, or account-linked data changes the analysis; the App Review explicit-permission rule for third-party AI and the privacy-policy requirement still need to be satisfied. ([App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.6 Conditional creator-content and age controls

If the service expands from a private user-to-AI interaction into a creator or community platform, Apple treats creator content—including audio—as user-generated content. In that conditional case, creator apps must let users identify content exceeding the app’s age rating and use a verified or declared age restriction mechanism to limit underage access, in addition to the §1.2 moderation/reporting/blocking requirements. This is not automatically triggered by a strictly private companion. ([App Review Guidelines §§1.2, 1.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.7 Voice-style simulation and Apple’s actual wording

The live Guidelines do not contain a dedicated “AI voice clone” section. Apple’s applicable text instead covers accurate descriptions and functionality, objectionable or harmful content, and third-party content that must be created by the developer or properly licensed; if the app uses or displays content from a third-party service, §5.2.2 also requires the relevant service’s authorization. A voice resembling a real person should therefore be treated as a rights/consent and representation question, not as an expressly stated Apple-specific voice-cloning rule. ([App Review Guidelines §§1.1.6, 2.3, 4.1, 5.2.1–5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.8 Commercial subscription details

Apple’s subscription rule requires ongoing value for an auto-renewable subscription, a subscription period of at least seven days, cross-device operation, clear pre-purchase disclosure of what the customer receives for the price, and compliant trial/offer presentation. Purchased In-App Purchase credits or currencies may not expire, and restorable purchases need a restore mechanism. These details matter if AI minutes, generated audio, or saved companion features are packaged as digital paid access. ([App Review Guidelines §§3.1.1–3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [StoreKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/implementing-a-store-in-your-app-using-the-storekit-api), [Apple subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/))

### 5.9 Store metadata, screenshots, and age rating

Apple’s metadata rules require the app description, screenshots, previews, and other marketing material to accurately represent the experience; where In-App Purchases are featured, the metadata must clearly indicate which items or subscriptions require an additional purchase. Screenshots, previews, and icons must be appropriate for a 4+ audience, and metadata materials must be used with the required rights and fictional account information rather than real-person account data. These rules matter if store assets depict chat threads, voices, or sleep-related results. ([App Review Guidelines §§2.3.1–2.3.2, 2.3.8–2.3.9](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata))

Apple’s App Store Connect age-rating definitions separately include medical or treatment information, health or wellness topics, messaging and chat, and broadly distributed user-generated content. A private AI exchange is not automatically user-to-user messaging or broadly distributed UGC, but the rating must follow the app’s actual capabilities and any publication/sharing path. ([Age-rating values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions))

### 5.10 Subscription contract disclosures

In addition to App Review §3.1.2, Apple’s Developer Program License Agreement Schedule 2 requires clear and conspicuous subscription title, duration, price (and, where appropriate, price per unit), and accessible Privacy Policy and Terms of Use; it also addresses fulfillment throughout the marketed period, price increases/consent, and In-App Purchase for subscription services. These contractual requirements should be reconciled with the StoreKit product configuration and the review build. ([Apple Developer Program License Agreement, Schedule 2 §§3.8–3.11](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/#S2), [App Review Guidelines §3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Setting up introductory offers](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions/))

### 5.11 Kids Category is conditional

If the app is primarily child-directed or is submitted in the Kids Category, Apple imposes additional parental-gate requirements around links, purchases, and distractions, and states that Kids Category apps may not send personally identifiable or device information to third parties and must comply with children’s privacy laws. This is conditional and does not automatically apply to a general-audience bedtime app, but age-rating and audience claims should match the actual product. ([App Review Guidelines — Kids Category](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#kids-category), [App Review Guidelines — Kids](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#kids))

### 5.12 Privacy-manifest implementation details

Apple’s current privacy-manifest guidance uses `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`. For an app or SDK that collects data, Apple’s technical note describes `NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes` entries with the data type, whether the data is linked to the user, whether it is used for tracking, and the applicable purposes. The manifest is not a substitute for the App Store Privacy questionnaire or the privacy policy. ([Adding a privacy manifest to your app or third-party SDK](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/adding-a-privacy-manifest-to-your-app-or-third-party-sdk), [TN3184: Adding data collection details to your privacy manifest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3184-adding-data-collection-details-to-your-privacy-manifest), [Manage App Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy/))

Apple’s required-reason API list is separate from microphone, Speech, and Photos permissions. The required-reason API categories Apple lists are ActiveKeyboards, DiskSpace, FileTimestamp, SystemBootTime, and UserDefaults; do not put ordinary microphone/Speech/Photos permission declarations into `NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes`. Each executable, dynamic library, or SDK using a required-reason API needs its own valid manifest, and invalid manifests can lead to App Store Connect rejection. ([TN3183: Adding required reason API entries to your privacy manifest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3183-adding-required-reason-api-entries-to-your-privacy-manifest), [Adding a privacy manifest to your app or third-party SDK](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/adding-a-privacy-manifest-to-your-app-or-third-party-sdk))

### 5.13 Apple tracking permission is narrower than AI processing

App Tracking Transparency concerns Apple’s definition of tracking across companies’ apps or websites, including cross-company linking for targeted advertising/measurement or data-broker sharing; ordinary user-requested processing by an AI service is not automatically ATT tracking. If the implementation does track, it must request authorization and provide `NSUserTrackingUsageDescription`. ([App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency), [`requestTrackingAuthorization`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency/attrackingmanager/requesttrackingauthorization(completionhandler:)), [`NSUserTrackingUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsusertrackingusagedescription), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/))

### 5.14 Personal Voice is not a voice-cloning license

Apple provides a Personal Voice authorization API for the system Personal Voice feature. That authorization does not constitute a legal license or consent to imitate a third party’s voice, and ordinary `AVSpeechSynthesizer` use does not answer the rights or disclosure questions for custom voice-style simulation. ([Request authorization to use a Personal Voice](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechsynthesizer/requestpersonalvoiceauthorization(completionhandler:)), [`AVSpeechSynthesizer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechsynthesizer), [App Review Guidelines §§1.1, 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.15 Health-data storage and iCloud

Apple’s health/privacy rule says personal health information must not be stored in iCloud and restricts the use of health and fitness data for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining outside the permitted purposes. If a sleep-time or sleep-quality inference is treated as personal health information, the storage and analytics design needs an explicit check against §5.1.3 rather than relying on “cloud minimized” language. ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 5.16 Data-flow inventory to keep product and legal review separate

The following matrix is an inventory template, not a prescribed architecture or a conclusion about any data item’s legal classification. For every row that exists in the implementation, record the collection point, purpose, recipient, off-device transmission, linkage, tracking status, retention, deletion propagation, and whether the vendor can independently reuse or train on the data. Those fields align with Apple’s App Privacy/Review disclosures and the GDPR/CCPA purpose, minimization, recipient, retention, and rights requirements. ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1–5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [GDPR Arts. 5, 13–14, 25, 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [California Civil Code §§1798.100, 1798.105, 1798.140](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&division=3.&title=1.81.5.&part=4.))

| Data object to inventory | Separate questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Raw microphone frames and saved recordings | Is capture live or retained; is it sent to Apple Speech or another vendor; what indication, permission, label, lawful basis, and deletion path apply? ([Apple Speech permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/asking-permission-to-use-speech-recognition), [App Review Guidelines §2.5.14](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) |
| Apple Speech request and recognition results | Is recognition on-device or server-based; are partial/final results retained; which Apple permission and usage string apply? ([`requiresOnDeviceRecognition`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognitionrequest/requiresondevicerecognition), [`NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information_property_list/nsspeechrecognitionusagedescription)) |
| Third-party AI/STT/TTS request payloads | What exact content leaves the device; is third-party AI sharing explicitly disclosed/authorized; does the provider discard, retain, train, or permit human access? ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) |
| Screenshot original, OCR text, extracted chat, and embeddings | Does the app have appropriate rights/authorization; are other people’s personal data or confidential messages present; which derivatives are retained and deleted? ([App Review Guidelines §§5.1.1, 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [GDPR Arts. 4–5, 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)) |
| Transcript history and generated audio | Are they user content, linked to an account, exported/shared, used for personalization, or retained in backups; what does deletion remove? ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), [GDPR Arts. 15–20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)) |
| Voice references, voiceprints, embeddings, and custom voice models | Is processing merely synthesis or does it attempt unique identification; what consent/license and vendor role apply; are models and derived features deletable? ([GDPR Arts. 4(14), 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [App Review Guidelines §5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) |
| Sleep-time, sleep-quality, mood, or health-related inferences | Is the output a generic time estimate or a health/profile inference; is it used for advertising, marketing, medical claims, or consequential decisions; where is it stored? ([App Review Guidelines §5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [GDPR Arts. 4(4), 4(15), 9, 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)) |
| Account/device IDs, logs, analytics, backups, and model-training copies | Can the data be re-linked; is it tracking; which SDK/vendor receives it; does deletion propagate through logs, backups, subprocessors, and independent training stores? ([Apple App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency), [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/), [GDPR Arts. 28, 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)) |
